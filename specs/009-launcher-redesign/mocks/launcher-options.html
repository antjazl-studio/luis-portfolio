009 Chat launcher: entry-point options (mock)

Static mock. 360px phone frames are real size; desktop frames are a 1280×720 viewport shown at 55%. Flat, tokens only, neutral controls; the only non-token colour is the WhatsApp brand green on the WhatsApp mark itself. Fonts are system-ui here (the site uses Geist), so text widths are close but not exact. The dashed line marks the Footer's bottom reserve (pb-36 = 144px) that keeps Footer icons clear of floating controls.

Spanish strings are the longest case; check they fit at 360px.

Today (for reference)

Outline "Ask about Luis" pill stacked 12px above a 48px green circle, same right margin. Two shapes, two weights, green dominates.

A. One dock, two segments

A single pill: assistant segment (icon + "Ask about Luis") and a WhatsApp icon segment, separated by a hairline. One outline, one height, one anchor point. The assistant segment is ~3x wider than the WhatsApp segment, so it leads.

Pros

  • One element, one anchor, one outline: fixes the "improvised stack" directly.
  • Assistant leads by width, icon and position; WhatsApp stays one tap away, never hidden.
  • No panel change, no new page content, no contract change; reuses the current launcher click handler and lazy panel.
  • Smallest footprint of the options that keep both visible (~200px wide at 360px).

Cons

  • Reads as a label, not an invitation to type; weaker "input" cue than B.
  • A1 neutral glyph is less recognisable as WhatsApp than the green; A2 brings green back.
  • Two tap targets in one pill: the 1px divider must be clear and each segment ≥44px.

Discoverability / thumb / conflicts

  • Discoverability: good: icon + words, bottom-right, always visible.
  • Thumb: bottom-right, right-thumb zone; ask segment is the closest, WhatsApp the farthest-right corner target.
  • Content/Footer: 64px of height at the bottom; fits inside the existing 144px reserve (no Footer change).

B. Input-like "Ask anything about Luis…" bar

A prominent input-looking bar. Mobile: bottom, full width minus 16px gutters. Desktop: bottom-right, 380px. Tapping it opens the panel with the textarea focused (a real page-level input would raise the keyboard before the panel exists; see cons). WhatsApp leaves the floating layer: Contact section + panel.

Pros

  • Strongest "type here" invitation; closest to the pattern the owner likes.
  • Single element; the assistant is unmistakably primary.
  • Frees the corner of the green dot entirely.

Cons

  • WhatsApp is demoted to 2+ taps (open panel first, or scroll to Contact). Recruiters who want WhatsApp lose the always-on shortcut.
  • Touches the panel: a WhatsApp link in the panel footer changes the panel (today WhatsApp shows only in error states), against "the panel stays as is". Needs an owner exception, or WhatsApp only goes to Contact.
  • Contact section gains a WhatsApp link (new content to design and translate).
  • Largest footprint: 328px wide on mobile, covers a row of content at all times.
  • A real input would summon the keyboard on the page, shift the viewport, and bypass the panel's lazy load; a look-alike button avoids that but is a "fake input" (fine when the tap opens a focused field).
  • Prefilled text needs a real input; the look-alike variant has nothing to prefill.

Discoverability / thumb / conflicts

  • Discoverability: highest for the assistant; lowest for WhatsApp.
  • Thumb: full-width bar is reachable one- or two-handed at the very bottom.
  • Content/Footer: 64px tall like A, but 328px wide on mobile; overlaps more text.

C. Assistant FAB with WhatsApp behind it

One round button. Tapping it reveals two actions above it: "Ask about Luis" and "WhatsApp"; the button becomes a close (X) control.

Pros

  • Smallest resting footprint (48px circle); the least page cover and the calmest page.
  • Single anchor point; both channels remain reachable.
  • No panel change.

Cons

  • Hides the most valuable feature behind an icon and then behind a second tap: assistant = 2 taps, WhatsApp = 2 taps. Opposite of "obvious primary entry point".
  • Icon-only on touch: no label until expanded; "AI sparkle" alone is ambiguous.
  • Expanded state needs open/close state, outside-click and Escape handling, focus management, and extra translated labels: the most new behaviour of the options.
  • Expanded stack overlaps Footer icons.

Discoverability / thumb / conflicts

  • Discoverability: lowest for the assistant.
  • Thumb: excellent; one fixed target, actions stack upward.
  • Content/Footer: collapsed fits; expanded exceeds the reserve temporarily.

D. Hybrid: A on mobile, an input-like wide segment on desktop

Same single dock as A. On mobile it is the compact A1 above. From the md breakpoint (768px) the assistant segment widens into an input-style field ("Ask anything about Luis…" with a hint chip) while the WhatsApp segment stays an icon. One component, no panel change, WhatsApp unchanged and always visible. The field is a button that opens the panel with the textarea focused.

Pros

  • Gets the "invites typing" cue where recruiters actually are (desktop), without B's costs.
  • Keeps A's single element and always-visible WhatsApp; no panel, Contact or Footer change.
  • Mobile stays compact (64px of height, ~200px wide).

Cons

  • Two layouts to verify (mobile dock, desktop field). The "AI" chip is extra copy (EN "AI" / ES "IA") and must prove it earns its place under "when in doubt, remove decoration".
  • A button styled as an input is a small affordance trade-off; the tap opens a focused field, so it is honest in practice.
  • Desktop footprint ≈ 400px wide at the bottom-right: wider than A.

Discoverability / thumb / conflicts

  • Discoverability: highest of the options that keep WhatsApp visible.
  • Thumb: same as A on phones; desktop is mouse-driven.
  • Content/Footer: fits the existing reserve on both.
